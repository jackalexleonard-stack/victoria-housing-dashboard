# Design review board — report (2026-07-21)

78 agents · 7 lenses · 42 findings raised · 38 survived adversarial verification (4 refuted).
Ground truth: live-site screenshots + metrics (desktop 13.5 screens, mobile 20.1, 27 charts).

## Overall assessment
The dashboard's core craft is strong — every chart leads with a plain-language finding, provenance is genuinely first-class, and the detail modal delivers real depth on demand — but the page-level defaults invert that craft exactly where the daily scan happens. The approved "lead finding card" design never shipped: the fold is five equal-weight stat tiles and a 15-chip strip that promises changes but shows only levels, so the product's best asset (27 computed finding sentences) is invisible on screen one of both viewports. Below the fold, one uniform always-expanded template repeated nine times, six full-height context charts in World, up-to-7-row metadata stacks under every chart, and an annotation picket fence stamped across five charts turn 27 charts into a 13.5–20 screen scroll. Almost all of this is fixable by restoring the approved design and changing defaults — very little content needs to be cut, and the brand identity survives every recommended change intact.

## The long-scroll verdict
Yes — your team's "information overload" report is real and measurable, but it is a defaults-and-hierarchy problem, not a content problem. It concentrates in four places: (1) the fold spends itself on five figure tiles and 15 delta-free chips with zero finding sentences, so the scan starts with no editorial lead; (2) roughly 45% of desktop page height (World, News, People, Social — 4,904px) is always-expanded context sitting below the core sections; (3) every one of 27 cards carries a permanent metadata stack (legends, source lines, notes) duplicating what the modal already holds; (4) annotation and legend ink on the Money/Prices charts out-weighs the data. The remedy's shape: restore the spec's lead-finding card at the top, make the change strip actually show changes, default-collapse World (and truncate News to its top stories) with state persisted, compact World to a tile row, and thin per-card chrome. Together that cuts the default mobile scroll by roughly 40–50% without deleting a single series — everything stays one tap away.

## Lens scores
### cognitive-load — 4/10
The card level is genuinely good — every chart leads with a plain-language finding and the detail modal delivers real depth-on-demand — but the page level fails "fast scan first": the first screen on both viewports shows five figure tickers and zero finding sentences, the what-changed strip omits the changes, and the answer to "what's the story today?" is scattered across 13.5–20 screens. The long scroll is not a content problem but a defaults problem: 45% of desktop page height (World, People, Social, News) is always-expanded low-priority context that the daily scan must physically pass.

### density-hierarchy — 4/10
The card anatomy, whitespace, and depth-on-demand modal are genuinely well crafted, but the page's hierarchy is inverted exactly where it matters: the fold shows five equal-weight figure tiles and a 15-chip wall with zero finding sentences (contradicting both the spec's lead-finding card and "findings, not figures"), and one uniform section template repeated nine times produces a 20-screen mobile scroll with no density contrast between core and context. Most of the overload is rhythm failure, not content volume — a restored lead finding, a truncated chip strip, one-line card footers, and a compact context-card size would cut the scroll by roughly a third without touching the brand.

### dataviz-craft — 4/10
The chart-card anatomy is genuinely good — finding-first titles, table toggles, source captions, one restrained palette — but the charts themselves repeatedly fail the reader: cash-rate event annotations are stamped as dashed-rule forests across four Money charts, mixed-scale series render the headline finding invisible (People, credit growth, Accord), and six-series tangles ignore the emphasis form their own findings call for. The World section spends six full-height charts (the largest section, 2,395px on the owner's phone) on single-series context that a sparkline tile row would serve better.

### type-color — 5/10
The Newsreader/Inter pairing is genuinely working mid-page — card findings read like a set publication, the amended warn token holds AA, and Inter carries controls and numerals cleanly. But the type-color system fails exactly where the daily scan happens: the fold is 100% Inter figures plus 34 look-alike pills with zero serif findings, clay has leaked from "editorial emphasis" into a picket fence of ~9px annotation labels that make the Money charts illegible on the owner's phone, and one pill shape now carries at least seven different meanings.

### mobile — 4/10
The mobile plumbing is genuinely first-class — tap-to-detail full-screen view, dvh/safe-area handling, a bottom-sheet filter dialog, pan-y charts — but the information hierarchy is a desktop page shrunk to one column: 20.08 screens, a fold that spends itself entirely on 5 oversized hero tiles with zero finding sentences, and every one of 9 sections expanded by default. The owner's daily phone scan currently requires marathon scrolling past annotation-carpeted, 6-legend charts and sub-30px touch targets, so the "under a minute" core job fails on the primary device.

### ux-copy — 6/10
The finding sentences themselves are the product's best copy — median 11 words, front-loaded "X fell Y% to Z in period" patterns that scan as data, not prose — but the words the owner actually sees first (ALL-CAPS jargon tile labels, delta-free "Changed this week" chips) betray the "findings, not figures" principle at the exact spot the daily scan starts. Below the fold, machine-vocabulary legends, a triple-repeated generic failure message, and "rose 0.0 pp" template glitches undercut an otherwise well-written editorial voice.

### economist-job — 6/10
The citation job is close to flawless — from the fold it is one chip-tap to Rents, one card-tap to a modal carrying the finding, stat block, linked source ID (ABS RES_DWELL_ST), vintage ("Data to Mar qtr 2026"), fetch recency, next-release estimate, scope chip, CSV and copy-link — and section order (prices→rents→supply→money→people→social→world) matches how an economist actually thinks. But the 60-second scan — the owner's stated core job — fails at the top: the fold shows five series-name tiles and a "Changed this week" strip with no deltas, the spec's approved lead-finding card never shipped, and narrating the week therefore requires reading 27 card findings across 13.5 desktop / 20 mobile screens, a third of which (rents) are 10 months stale behind an unexplained "4 sources unavailable" chip.

## Prioritized findings

### [P0] Ship the approved lead-finding card — the fold currently leads with figures, not findings (effort M; lenses: cognitive-load, density-hierarchy, type-color, ux-copy, economist-job)
**Evidence:** Spec §3 approved "Today briefing — lead finding (large card), two secondary findings"; the live build ships five equal-weight ALL-CAPS stat tiles instead. metrics.json: first_finding_visible=false on BOTH viewports while hero_tiles_visible=5/5; the first Newsreader sentence sits at offsetTop 755 desktop / 1687 mobile. All five text-analysis lenses independently flagged this as their top finding.

**Change:** Restore the spec'd Today layout: one large Newsreader lead-finding card (sourced from the precomputed hero picks — the sentences already exist in site.json via the TILE_CHART key→chart mapping), two secondary finding cards, and the five stat tiles compressed into one compact static Inter strip beneath (keep cash rate and Melb dwelling values prominent — they are deliberate pins your team checks daily; keep staleness chips and tap-to-detail on each). On mobile use the zero-net-height variant (lead finding replaces the first tile row) so nothing pushes further below the fold. Recase tile labels to sentence case and move (MOM)/(YR) codes into the delta line. Note: the export currently ships 5 tiles, not 3 picks, so either pin lead=tile 1 or add a small scoring field in pipeline/export.py; precomputed picks apply to the default unfiltered view only — revert to tiles when filters are active. Acceptance test: one full finding sentence visible at scroll 0 at 393px (add to the Playwright suite).

### [P0] "Changed this week" must show changes: render the deltas it already computes, then rank and cap the strip (effort S; lenses: cognitive-load, density-hierarchy, mobile, ux-copy, economist-job)
**Evidence:** All 15 chips show name + current level only ("Median rent — Melbourne $575/wk") with zero arrows or signed deltas, while the hero tiles directly above prove the delta treatment exists. The pipeline already exports delta and delta_color per chip; TodaySection.tsx (lines 19–27) simply drops both. On mobile the strip stacks ~14 rows (~570–600px, ~0.7 of a screen) between the tiles and Top stories. Five lenses converged on this.

**Change:** Step 1 (few-line frontend fix): render the already-exported t.delta via the existing TILE_FMT[key].delta formatter, colored by delta_color with the +/− sign as the non-color cue, matching HeroTiles exactly; null deltas fall back to level-only. Step 2: rank by the existing pipeline/scoring.py export-time scores, show the top ~5–6 with an inline "and 9 more changes" disclosure (mirroring NewsSection's existing "Show all N" pattern — not a horizontal scroll row, which hides items). Keep pale-tint chips, never solid fills. Badge any source-unavailable series in the strip with its vintage (see the stale-rents item) rather than excluding it.

### [P0] Default-collapse the context tail (World; truncate News to top stories) and flip mobile to collapsed-by-default (effort M; lenses: cognitive-load, mobile, density-hierarchy)
**Evidence:** All 9 sections default expanded though collapse machinery already exists (chevron + vh.collapsed localStorage in web/src/App.tsx). World (1,523px desktop / 2,395px mobile — the largest chart section, bigger than Prices) + News (2,491/3,283px) + People + Social = 45% of desktop page height. Mobile total: 17,107px = 20.08 screens. This is the direct answer to the team's reported complaint.

**Change:** Desktop: default-collapse World; truncate News to its 4 top stories with the existing "show all 148" expander (News is the page's only daily-fresh section — don't hide it behind a bare header); merge or shrink People+Social (only ~8% of the page — low win, don't count on them). Mobile (coarse-pointer only): default every section after Today to collapsed, keeping section order and the one-page IA. Collapsed rows get a Newsreader finding-style summary line ("World — iron ore extends its slide", generated by the same findings.py rules), never a "6 series · -7.2%" ticker string, and must surface the section's worst status chip so outages stay visible. Persist overrides as explicit per-section open/closed deltas against the viewport default (the current vh.collapsed semantics don't survive a viewport-dependent default); ?s= deep-links and scrollspy taps always auto-expand. Ship the mobile default flip first (S); the per-chart finding-TOC inside collapsed sections is a second, separately-reviewed increment. Also demote Brent crude from World's full-width lead slot.

### [P0] Delete the cash-rate annotation picket fence from five charts; keep the story, lose the forest (effort S; lenses: cognitive-load, density-hierarchy, dataviz-craft, type-color, mobile)
**Evidence:** ~19–20 dashed clay markers with stacked ±0.xx labels carpet the cash-rate card and are duplicated wholesale onto owner-occupier lending, housing credit growth, and both HVI price cards — labels collide and clip mid-glyph ("+0", "-0") at card edges; on mobile the fence covers over half the plot width and out-inks the data. **Change:** On the cash-rate card, drop the fence entirely — the plotted step line already shows every move (optionally keep one small label on the most recent move, since there the annotation is the story). On lending, credit-growth, and both HVI cards, replace per-move rules with a single pale-clay-tint shaded band marking the tightening/easing cycle (explained once in the caption), or unlabelled major-turning-point ticks only — no per-move labels at card size, at any breakpoint (the collisions exist on desktop too, so make label handling density-aware rather than a mobile-only gate). The fully labelled event set lives in the detail modal at ≥12px Inter with collision-avoiding stagger; keep the static Accord start-date marker labelled wherever it appears. Restyle markers to spec: thin solid clay, not dashed orange.

## Considered and rejected

- **Filter hero-duplicate chips out of the "Changed this week" strip** — The duplication is signal, not noise — a chip's presence tells the reader new data landed this week, which the permanent hero tiles cannot convey; filtering would make the changelog silently incomplete and saves only ~40px on a 10,816px page.
- **Raise all 27 finding headlines a type step so sentences always outweigh numbers** — Within every card the hierarchy is already correct (finding largest, serif, top); the "inversion" only exists comparing sections 600px apart, and larger headlines would add wrap lines to an already 20-screen mobile page.
- **Make every text link blue to enforce blue=interactive** — The rule constrains what may be blue, not what must be; muted-underline is a deliberate de-emphasis grammar for provenance links, a labeled Source button already covers the task, and blue-ifying 148 news links would scatter noise down the exact scroll the team complained about.
- **Compact the mobile hero tiles to single-line rows to get a finding above the fold** — The screenshot contradicts the premise (the mobile fold already shows six changed-this-week chips below the tiles), the pixel math was in dpr-2 image pixels so the reclaimed height cannot bring the first finding (offsetTop 1687px) above an 852px fold, and the tile compaction saves ~1.5% of the 17,107px scroll.
